Mission

For the people
in the room.

We make software for communities, players and friend groups. Not for metrics, not for noise, not for the graph. If a thing is good for the room and bad for the chart, we build it anyway.

What we hold to

Four things, and we mean all four.

These are not values on a wall. They are the arguments we settle decisions with, and they are the reason some obvious features never got built.

01 — Social

The internet is at its best as a group chat.

We design for the people inside the room, not for the algorithm watching them. The measure of a feature is whether the conversation got better, not whether the session got longer.

02 — Creative

Playful, colourful, a little odd on purpose.

Software should feel like somebody cared while making it. That is why our products have characters, why the shapes are hand-placed, and why nothing here looks like a template.

03 — Practical

No dark patterns. No engagement traps.

Leaving is as easy as arriving, the off switch is where you would look for it, and we do not dress a decision up to push you toward the answer that suits us.

04 — European

No quarterly graph to please.

We are independent and based in France. Nobody is waiting on a number from us this quarter, so we move at the speed of getting it right rather than the speed of getting it announced.

The whole idea

Make It Matter.

Three words, and they are a test rather than a slogan. If a thing we are about to ship would not matter to the person on the other end of it, it does not go out.

The cost of it

What that rules out.

A promise is only worth reading if it says what it gives up. These are things we could do, that would work, and that we are not doing.

An engagement feed

A ranked, endless feed is the most reliable way to grow the numbers and the most reliable way to make a room worse. Our products show you what the people you chose are saying, in the order they said it.

Selling what you say to each other

Messages between people are not inventory. Lifecheck exists partly because the usual way of proving someone is human involves watching them across the rest of the web, and we did not want to do that to our own visitors either.

Passing AI off as a person

Supernova labels what it writes. Not in a footnote, but where you read it. An assistant that is useful does not need to be mistaken for a human to be worth having.

Announcing things that do not exist

Hereld is in build, and every page that mentions it says exactly that: no waiting list, no countdown, no render of a screen nobody can open yet. When it is ready, that is when it gets a launch.

Fortized was the first thing we shipped.

A community platform for gamers and the friend groups around them. Whatever ships next follows the same compass.
